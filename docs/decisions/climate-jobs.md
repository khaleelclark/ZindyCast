# H3 long-baseline jobs — implementation proposal

September 11, 2026. **Design only; not implemented or scientifically validated.** Scope: requested ERA5, 2–5 cities, 1991–2020 and the candidate recent decade 2016–2025, preserving the existing 1–366-day custom comparison. No service, database, provider, deployment, or dependency changes are authorized by this document. All numerical resource limits below are proposed application bounds, not provider promises or heat thresholds.

## Evidence and present constraints

Read [implementation plan](../implementation-plan.md), [foundation](foundation.md), [station research](../research/station-history.md), [preserved evidence index](../research/history/README.md), and the current [comparison implementation](../../packages/comparison/src/index.ts), [contracts](../../packages/contracts/src/comparison.ts), [history adapter](../../packages/history/src/index.ts), [worker](../../apps/worker/src/comparison-worker.ts), [storage](../../packages/storage/src/index.ts), and [jobs](../../packages/jobs/src/index.ts). This review made no network requests. External links in existing research retain their original audit dates; provider terms must be revalidated before enabling the new workload.

Current custom mode uses five fields, UTC dates, requested `era5`, land-cell selection and provider default elevation adjustment. Each upstream request is at most 31 inclusive days, 744 rows and 1,000,000 response bytes, with an eight-second adapter timeout and ten-second worker limit. The worker retains all chunks, then aggregates, and limits an attempt to 15 minutes. Its cache TTL is one day; shared cache defaults are 1,000 entries, 32 MiB JSON, 2 MiB/entry and 128 MiB physical admission. Jobs default to 1,000 rows, 2 MiB per payload/result, 32 MiB combined JSON, 128 MiB physical admission, maximum 30-day expiry and 1–10 explicit attempts. Those are not long-baseline storage or scheduling contracts.

Shared Open-Meteo accounting currently reserves `ceil(inclusiveDays / 14)` for these five-field requests before sending. Configured rolling limits are 600/minute, 5,000/hour, 10,000/day and 300,000/31 days; forecast, geocoding and history share the provider identity. Failed requests are not refunded. Existing code has no background class reservation, checkpoint storage, suspend-without-attempt-consumption operation or result pagination. Merely increasing the date/schema limit would exceed runtime/memory budgets and could repeatedly refetch evicted chunks.

Station evidence establishes neither a complete hourly baseline nor interchangeable station/model data. Nine daily records and five January hourly prefixes were audited; station inventory endpoints are not completeness. Daily measurement flag P (missing presumed zero), trace rainfall, source-specific QC, station relocations and unresolved observation-day conventions remain material. The St. George metadata conflict is unresolved. No station substitution, station splicing, daily-to-hourly conversion or station WBGT is part of this ERA5 job. Requested ERA5 still has `constituent:null`, unknown source issue/update times and potentially revised data; fetching a fixed model does not prove an immutable homogeneous climate record.

## Product definition and scientific eligibility

Add a new product rather than changing `comparison-v1`. Initial long product always returns both windows from one frozen union plan. Query shape: `{version:'era5-climate-query-v1', locations:ComparisonLocation[2..5], selection:'baseline-and-recent', calendar:'UTC'}`. No arbitrary dates, model, variables, URLs, heat policy, tile list or client job ID. The server freezes `resolvedAt`, `candidateEndYear`, both explicit windows, closing precipitation time, tile bounds and all method versions in the persisted payload. On this design date the windows are 1991-01-01 through 2020-12-31 and 2016-01-01 through 2025-12-31.

For later years, start from UTC current year minus one and require the entire canonical fetch extent, including closing precipitation and tile overfetch, to be within the adapter's current five-day availability bound. If the latest calendar candidate is not requestable, report that distinction and require an explicitly labeled earlier common window; do not silently slide different cities to different periods. Validate availability again before dispatch. Freeze the chosen decade for retries; a new year does not mutate an old job.

Three independent facts must be returned:

- `calendarComplete`: every requested calendar year has ended; not a statement about records.
- `coverageStatus` per field/bucket: complete, partial or no_data against every expected slot. Absent source hours and present-null values remain separate. A successful empty provider response can produce a completed job with no_data coverage.
- `scientificEligibility`: `descriptive_only` initially, with reason codes for missing data, unknown revision/homogeneity, unsupported metric or unresolved method policy. Full counts do not certify a climatological normal or a scientifically representative trend. A future eligibility policy gets a separate version; do not invent a percent-coverage cutoff now.

Use “1991–2020 ERA5 descriptive baseline” and “2016–2025 ERA5 descriptive comparison,” not certified station normals. Means/min/max/distributions of available values show their denominator. Complete precipitation total requires every interval; otherwise only `sumAvailableMm` is shown, never scaled to a year. Do not rank a city as improved from unequal missingness. Show both individual-support statistics and common-support statistics over exactly the same non-null hourly slots across every selected city, separately per field and bucket. Even common support remains a selected subset and may be seasonally biased. Baseline and recent overlap in 2016–2020; they are not independent samples, and this product supplies no significance/causal claims.

Calendar convention is UTC Gregorian throughout this version, labeled in charts and exports; leap days retain equal hourly weight. This is not a local-night or local-day climate product. Year buckets contain January–December. Month buckets include each year-month plus pooled January, February, etc. Season year Y uses DJF = December Y−1 through February Y, MAM = March–May Y, JJA = June–August Y, SON = September–November Y. Fetch December 1990 and December 2015 for the first winters. Annual/monthly results exclude those supporting dates; season results use them explicitly. Consequently the four season-year buckets do not partition the same January–December year; never reconcile annual totals by summing those four seasons. All 30/10 complete season-year windows exist in the plan, independent of their actual data coverage.

For instantaneous fields use `[start,end)` samples. Rain uses preceding-hour intervals with endpoints `start+1h ... end` and is bucketed by interval start. Retain the closing endpoint at January 1, 2021/2026 and tile-boundary carry; discard every other overfetch sample. Daily minimum/maximum temperature could be a later descriptive UTC-day product with daily counts, but is not “warm nights.” Warm-night counts, humid-condition exceedances, exposure counts and WBGT remain unavailable pending approved definitions and the expanded, validated radiation/pressure/wind/heat input method. Do not derive heat from monthly or daily means.

## Bounded streaming algorithm and distributions

Use canonical 28-day UTC tiles anchored at 1990-12-01. A tile is a whole inclusive 28-day request, independent of selected cities/windows; key its exact dates and requested coordinates using the existing `history:era5:5fields:utc:v1:` canonical query property order. Never alias an existing 31-day cache entry as a 28-day one. Compatible custom queries may hit the same exact key; arbitrary overlap does not imply a cache hit. A new tile/aggregation version belongs in checkpoint identity, not a misleading upstream cache alias.

Process tiles in chronological order and cities sequentially inside each tile. Hold at most one normalized tile per city (5 × 672 rows) to compute all-city common support; never retain the whole series. Preserve one boundary row per city so precipitation endpoints are assigned exactly once. Validate fixed URL/model/units/query/timeline, retrieval age, source grid/elevation and provenance on both cache and provider paths. Persist the first grid identity and reject changes within a location across either window; different requested cities may share one grid and must be labeled as such. Never deduplicate city identities merely because returned grids coincide.

Maintain compensated sums, counts, min/max, missing partitions, and gap state (`prefixGap`, `suffixGap`, `maxGap`, first/last valid time) per field/bucket; update in fixed chronological order. Longest gaps must join correctly across tiles and missing months. Maintain individual and common-support summaries, whose means use their own counts. Expected counts come from the canonical calendar, not provider row count. Persist support semantics in the result; never imply the current v1 individual mean is computed on common support.

Proposed first distribution method: fixed 256-bin histograms per field with exact bin counts, plus exact count/min/max/compensated sum. Histogram edges are a versioned statistical representation, never heat categories. Start with linear edges spanning the existing validated field ranges (temperature/dew point −150..80 °C, RH 0..100%, wind 0..200 m/s, precipitation 0..3000 mm), lower-inclusive/upper-exclusive except the last inclusive upper bound. Store missing counts outside bins; zeros, including rain zeros, remain valid. Counts sum exactly to validCount. Persist counts as unsigned 32-bit binary values; maximum expected period hours is far below their capacity.

Return empirical CDF bins and optional quantile **intervals**, not invented exact percentiles: for p in {0.05,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.95}, use nearest-rank `ceil(p*n)` and return the containing bin's [lower,upper] bounds (null for n=0). This retains a deterministic value-width bound: 0.8984375 °C, 0.390625 percentage points RH, 0.78125 m/s and 11.71875 mm. Rain bins are likely too coarse for useful small-amount distributions: usability review is a release gate, with a separately versioned nonuniform edge table or bounded exact external-sort method as alternatives. Do not silently switch algorithms or call interval midpoints exact medians. Histograms are pooled hourly distributions, not distributions of annual means; neither are averages of per-tile quantiles. Expose the sample unit and distribution method.

Provide buckets for each selected year, each year-month, each season-year and, for each window, whole-window plus 12 pooled months and four pooled seasons: `40*(1+12+4) + 2*(1+12+4) = 714` buckets per city. Overlap is intentionally represented in both windows. Aggregate versions/schema must support both individual and common support. Common-support counts are shared across cities but values/distributions are per city.

## Exact request budgets

Arithmetic below was checked offline with Gregorian UTC dates. One cold tile/city is one HTTP request, at most 1,000,000 accepted body bytes, 672 rows and weight 2. Response caps count decoded bytes read by the adapter, not compressed network-wire size; rejected oversized transfers can deliver an overshooting stream chunk before abort. Parsed object memory is not bounded by body bytes alone.

| Scope (including first DJF and closing endpoint) | Tiles/city | Fetched dates | Calls, 2 cities | Calls, 5 cities | Weight, 2 cities | Weight, 5 cities |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline only, planning comparison | 393 | 1990-12-01..2021-01-15 | 786 | 1,965 | 1,572 | 3,930 |
| Recent only, planning comparison | 132 | 2015-11-28..2026-01-09 | 264 | 660 | 528 | 1,320 |
| Both, proposed single union job | 458 | 1990-12-01..2026-01-09 | 916 | 2,290 | 1,832 | 4,580 |

The separate-scope rows explain costs; initial public query offers the union only. Union saves 67 tile/city requests versus separate baselines. Requested annual windows contain 10,958 and 3,653 days (baseline 262,992 hours; recent 87,672). Union fetches 12,824 days/city, 307,776 rows/city; five cities = 1,538,880 rows and 7,694,400 numeric field slots streamed, never held simultaneously. The larger fetched range covers padding; it does not enlarge annual denominators.

Cold accepted body ceilings are 916 MB / 2,290 MB decimal (about 873.6 / 2,183.9 MiB) for 2/5 cities. Baseline alone is 786/1,965 MB and recent alone 264/660 MB. At ten seconds per completed request, sequential request-time envelopes are 9,160 s (2h32m40s) / 22,900 s (6h21m40s) for the union; baseline 7,860/19,650 s and recent 2,640/6,600 s. These are request-time sums, not successful-job duration guarantees: CPU, SQLite, queueing, quota and provider failures add time, and timeout fails the request. The existing 15-minute attempt cannot serve this product.

With fresh matching raw cache for all tiles: zero provider calls/weight, same aggregation work. Full explicit refresh costs another cold pass and creates a new result revision; never background-refresh decades because someone opened a result. A durable checkpoint resumes after committed tiles even when one-day raw cache entries have expired. With three allowed attempts, a conservative no-reuse ceiling is 6,870 calls, 13,740 weight and 6,870 MB accepted bodies for five cities; useful checkpoint reuse reduces this. A crash after charge/send but before durable tile commit can repeat at most five city calls for that tile per recovery (weight 10, up to 5 MB), assuming previous checkpoints remain valid. No refund and no exactly-once claim. Cap total charged weight per job at three cold passes, persist before network, and do not reset it on recovery.

## Foreground headroom, scheduling and admission

Propose background climate ceilings 60/minute, 500/hour, 2,000/day and 60,000/rolling31days, while enforcing existing shared limits in the **same quota transaction**. This leaves at least 540/minute, 4,500/hour, 8,000/day and 240,000/31days of each nominal budget unconsumed by climate work alone. Foreground can still exhaust the shared total; this is headroom, not a service guarantee. Never implement the class as a second provider bucket disconnected from `open-meteo`, nor as a racy check-then-reserve. Reserve both ledgers before every attempt; failures/cancellation do not refund. Class allowance, 429 Retry-After, cache lease contention or capacity failure must not trigger an unbudgeted alternate fetch.

At 500 weight/hour, a cold five-city union needs 9.16 hours of allowance; at 2,000/day it spans at least three daily allowance batches (third begins no earlier than about 48 hours after a burst-first start). Two cities fit the daily allowance but need 3.664 hours of hourly allowance. These are throughput/batch calculations, not ETAs: rolling windows, foreground use and provider availability dominate. Three worst-case passes at five cities consume 13,740 weight, seven daily batches. Set job expiry 14 days (within credential lifetime), explicit maxAttempts 3, and show no completion promise; quota waits do not consume attempts. If completion cannot fit the remaining expiry, expose a structured expiry/budget result rather than silently extending ownership.

One active climate job globally for execution, maximum two admitted nonterminal climate jobs globally and one per installation, in addition to existing installation active-job limits. Reserve worst-case durable storage at admission, not after downloading. Scheduler yields between time tiles (at most 5 sequential requests, about 50 seconds of request budget), selects waiting custom jobs first, then climate jobs round-robin by installation; no spinning/sleeping with a running lease for multi-hour quota waits. Proposal adds fenced `suspend(lease,availableAt,reason)` to queued plus a persisted `resumePending` marker: resuming a voluntary suspension retains the attempt number, resets no durable progress and issues a new lease token; recovering an abandoned running lease increments attempt as today. Failed jobs never automatically retry. Queued quota wait remains cancellable/owned, and cleanup expiry remains final.

Keep 30-second job leases and current cancellation/revocation polling with renewal; periodically yield CPU during aggregation to allow polling. Bound each dispatch slice to five requests and 60 seconds wall time, checkpoint before voluntary suspension. For a stall, abort and fail or leave recoverable state according to shutdown semantics. Progress is committed tile count / 458, monotonic across voluntary suspensions and recovery because it represents durable work; current custom per-attempt progress stays unchanged.

## Durable storage and schema changes (lead-owned migrations)

Keep exactly the existing three databases. Put climate checkpoints/result pages in jobs.sqlite, never in the disposable coordination cache. Do not store bearer credentials in payloads/pages. Raw history cache keeps existing keys/one-day freshness and normal eviction; it cannot guarantee raw retention for recovery. Reserve a climate raw-cache class of at most 8 MiB/16 entries under the existing 32 MiB total so background history cannot evict all foreground records. SharedStorage currently has no class cap/partition: this is an explicit migration/API requirement. Validate each normalized tile against a proposed 512 KiB serialization cap before cache insertion/checkpoint use; exceeding it fails closed. At most five such tiles = 2.5 MiB serialized working data, but JS heap/RSS needs measurement.

Proposed durable layout and APIs, names to coordinate before implementation:

1. Contracts: add `ClimateQuerySchema`, `ClimatePlanSchema`, `ClimateJobSchema`, `ClimateResultManifestSchema`, `ClimateResultPageSchema`, `ClimateBucketSchema`, `ClimateDistributionSchema` and schema-derived types. Literal job kind `climate-v1`, calculation `era5-climate-v1`, calendar/coverage/histogram/tile versions explicit. Strict cross-field validation checks canonical plan hash, bucket bounds/count partitions, quantile intervals, source identity and support denominator. Existing Comparison* and Reanalysis* limits remain intact; no v1 backfill or reinterpretation.
2. Jobs migration: additive `zc_climate_checkpoint` keyed by job ID with FK cascade, frozen plan hash, cursor, grid identities, boundary carry, cumulative charged budget, checkpoint version and revision; `zc_climate_pages` keyed by (jobId, revision, pageId) with encoding, byte count, SHA-256 and bounded BLOB/JSON content. Extend admission byte accounting to include these tables and reserved bytes. Add `commitClimateTile(lease,expectedCursor,nextCursor,pageUpdates,carry,provenance)` in one BEGIN IMMEDIATE transaction, atomically checking current unexpired lease/owner/attempt/job state, advancing cursor and updating affected aggregate pages. Duplicate/stale commits return false. Readers cannot see partly updated pages as a completed result.
3. Publication: `completeClimate(lease,manifest)` validates every expected page/count/hash and atomically sets terminal state + immutable revision. Job.result holds a manifest <=64 KiB, not all hourly rows/distributions. Page queries require authorization on every request, match published revision, and never accept arbitrary filenames/SQL keys. Suggested REST resources: POST `/api/v1/climate-jobs`, GET/DELETE `/api/v1/climate-jobs/:id`, GET `/api/v1/climate-jobs/:id/result/pages/:pageId`; lead chooses final route names. CSV streams authorized immutable pages, repeats source/time/count/method metadata and spreadsheet-escapes text; it does not silently start another archive download for raw-hour export.
4. Quota migration: class identity on quota events, atomic shared+class reservation and persisted config version; global/per-installation climate admission reservations need a race-safe owner record in installations.sqlite or an equivalent trusted reservation transaction. Retain reserve-before-enqueue, never-reused server IDs and conservative absent-orphan expiry. No cross-DB atomicity is claimed. Before shared quota reservation, durably debit the per-job attempt budget under the job lease; then reserve shared/class quota and send. A crash or denial between these transactions may overcharge the job budget, which is conservative and must be visible; never refund ambiguous work. Shared quota denial causes no send. Recovery cannot reset the per-job budget merely because the other database committed first.
5. Scheduling migration: persisted suspension marker/reason/availableAt and explicit attempt semantics; versions must fence old workers that do not understand climate pages, class quota or suspension. Retain terminal admission closure before release. Installations enumeration/reconciliation checks confirmed terminal state; queued/running/ambiguous absent jobs retain admission. Cancellation/revocation blocks further writes and network work; pages of cancelled/expired jobs are not exposed as successful output.

Storage arithmetic includes both support families: 714 buckets × 5 cities × 5 fields × 2 supports × (1,024 histogram bytes + proposed 256 compact scalar bytes) = **45,696,000 bytes** (43.58 MiB) maximum aggregate encoding. Add at most 2 KiB provenance/tile/city × 2,290 = 4,689,920 bytes, <=1 MiB manifest/cursor/carry/index metadata, and page overhead: reserve **64 MiB/job**. Encoding/schema must enforce those caps; JSON object size cannot be assumed to equal compact binary size. Provenance pages keep exact query, retrieval timestamp, grid/elevation, request URL and adapter version; no multi-megabyte source list inside the job manifest. Pages <=64 KiB decoded response require bounded pagination; count actual serialized bytes on output.

Two admitted/retained climate jobs reserve 128 MiB; propose climate durable cap 128 MiB, existing ordinary jobs JSON cap 32 MiB and jobs DB physical admission cap **384 MiB**, preserving space for indexes/freelist/WAL. Completed retained climate jobs count toward the two storage reservations until explicit deletion/expiry (expiry 14 days); execution slots can free at terminal state. Do not market 384 MiB as a hard disk bound: long readers can pin WAL and admission/page ceilings are imperfect. Include WAL/SHM in checks, bound readers, checkpoint, fail closed on disk/busy/size errors, and verify real RSS below the existing proposed 512 MiB worker limit. Avoid rewriting a 64 MiB checkpoint every tile: update only affected bounded bucket pages and cursor atomically; measure write amplification before release.

Persisted repositories currently require identical configuration on all connections; changing caps cannot be rolled out by changing one process's constructor. Lead must implement explicit schema/config migration and coordinated API+worker stop/backup/update/restart, with no mixed-version or independent rollback. Back up all three DBs consistently using existing operations procedure; quota ledger must not be rewound then used for fetching. No deployment action is performed by this design.

## Acceptance slices and gates

1. **Pure plan/contracts and stream engine:** lead publishes schemas and golden synthetic calendars first; bounded comparison owner implements canonical tiles, bucket assignment, scalar/histogram/common-support aggregation. Test Gregorian leap years, both supporting Decembers, all closing rain endpoints, adjacent tile boundaries, all-null/absent/partial fields, grid changes, duplicate chunks and exact v1 custom regression. Compare tiny streamed runs against independently computed direct arrays; reconcile histogram counts, common denominators and longest gaps. Fix encoding and histogram usability before locking versions.
2. **Persistence and quota:** lead owns migrations and tests independent connections for class/shared quota race, admission/storage reservation race, fenced tile commit, suspend/resume attempt semantics, and atomic publication. Inject crashes before/after send, charge, checkpoint, terminal commit and admission release; committed cursor never double-counts or resets cost. Test corrupted hashes/pages, stale writers, rollback clocks, explicit retry ceiling, quota-event capacity, disk-full/long-reader WAL and old-version rejection. Crash tests should include actual child-process termination, not only orderly close.
3. **Worker integration:** synthetic five-city 458-tile run only, no provider fetches until separately approved bounded evidence. Test cancel/revoke/delete/expire during fetch and aggregation, shutdown leaving recoverable lease/admission, competing lease takeover, queued wait cancellation, foreground precedence, fair tile scheduling, max budget/expiry, full raw-cache eviction and restart from durable checkpoint. A cancelled job cannot publish or become readable through guessed page IDs. Absent reservation-before-enqueue race remains charged until safely reconciled/expired. Record peak RSS, total bytes, write amplification, slice latency and completion under mock delays.
4. **REST/UI/export:** installation auth on every detail/page/export/cancel; result revision stable across pagination; cross-installation access denied; no bearer/cache in service worker or logs. Show candidate dates versus coverage versus eligibility, UTC and DJF labels, both denominators, modeled source/grid, missingness, overlapping periods, quantile intervals, progress/wait reason and expiration. No heat defaults or claim of full H3 completion while exposure definitions remain unavailable. Bound text/CSV/output pages and UI bucket selection; test abandoned browser and resumed retrieval.
5. **Release review:** rerun typecheck/build/relevant node:test suites after implementation in an authorized work interval. Before any real long job, revalidate provider weighted charging/terms and shared identity limits from authoritative sources, review the above caps/migrations and distribution precision, obtain lead's quota-safe bounded live-check scope, then measure actual deployment host behavior. Device rendering and operational recovery remain separate gates. Full warm-night/humid/heat measures require sourced method/policy and new inputs/versions; they are not delivered by five-field climate statistics.

Validation of this design: offline date/tile/request arithmetic and relative-link checks only. No application build was run because a build writes served static assets outside this doc's authorized scope; no tests generating evidence outside this document, provider calls, services, persistent DBs or deployment files were changed.
